# CrisisTrust Developers & Community

> **Original creator, project owner, and primary maintainer: Chris Cruz | h4ckd4d**

CrisisTrust is intended to become an open, professionally maintained public-interest technology project.

## Who we want to collaborate with

We welcome:

- software and mobile developers;
- CAP and emergency-alerting implementers;
- privacy/security engineers;
- humanitarian technologists;
- disaster-risk and community-resilience specialists;
- accessibility and inclusive-design specialists;
- multilingual/localization contributors;
- UX researchers;
- QA/test engineers;
- protocol and data-model engineers;
- technical writers;
- NGO and civic-technology practitioners.

## Priority engineering areas

### Provenance and integrity

Make source attribution understandable, auditable, and resistant to impersonation without turning trust into a simplistic badge.

### Humanitarian UX

Design for stress, limited attention, low bandwidth, accessibility needs, and multilingual environments.

### Privacy-preserving coordination

Enable useful check-ins and trusted-circle coordination without continuous tracking, advertising identifiers, or unnecessary personal data.

### CAP interoperability

Develop tested adapters and mappings that preserve CAP semantics rather than rewriting official safety guidance.

### Community resource quality

Improve freshness, verification, moderation, accessibility, and source history for public support resources.

### Offline resilience

Research synchronization patterns for degraded connectivity without silently weakening privacy.

## Engineering review standard

Changes should answer:

- What failure during a crisis does this prevent?
- What uncertainty is visible to the user?
- What data is collected?
- How long is it retained?
- Does it create a network dependency?
- Can it be abused for surveillance or misinformation?
- How is it tested?
- What happens when connectivity is unavailable?
