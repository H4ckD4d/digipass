# Family Digital Safety Playbook

> **Project owner: Chris Cruz | h4ckd4d**

This playbook is for collaborative family safety planning. It is not a covert-monitoring guide.

## 1. Agree on the purpose

Discuss what the family is trying to improve: account security, privacy settings, unwanted contact, safer sharing, or a trusted response plan.

## 2. Define trusted contacts

Agree in advance who a young person can contact when something online feels confusing, threatening, persistent, or uncomfortable. A trusted adult should focus on safety and support rather than blame.

## 3. Review account protections together

For important accounts:

- enable strong MFA where available;
- review recovery methods;
- remove unknown sessions/devices;
- review privacy and audience settings;
- turn on useful login/security alerts.

Never put authentication secrets into the DSI assessment.

## 4. Minimize location and routine exposure

Review whether apps or posts expose precise location, school details, regular routes, or real-time routines unnecessarily. Prefer sharing the minimum needed with the intended audience.

## 5. Plan for unwanted contact

Agree on a simple response:

1. stop engaging when appropriate;
2. use block/mute controls;
3. use the platform's reporting and safety tools;
4. preserve only minimal relevant evidence;
5. involve a trusted adult or appropriate professional/lawful channel if the situation is persistent or concerning.

Do not independently investigate or confront another person.

## 6. Practice account recovery

Know how to recover important accounts without sharing passwords or recovery codes with other people.

## 7. Revisit the plan

Digital-safety plans should evolve as apps, devices, accounts, and family needs change.
